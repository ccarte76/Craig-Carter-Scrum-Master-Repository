# Sprint 1 — Core Checkout

### Sprint Goal

Improve the core checkout experience by making order review, payment failure handling, and order confirmation clearer and more reliable.

### Results

The team completed 16 of 21 planned story points.

### Key Impediments

* Payment service dependency
* Environment/access issues
* Requirement ambiguity

### Scrum Master Actions

I made the payment dependency visible, identified the appropriate dependency owner, facilitated communication, and followed up until the required information was available.

During the Retrospective, the team also identified unclear requirements as an area for improvement.

### Improvement Actions

The team agreed to:

* Improve refinement before Sprint Planning
* Resolve access issues earlier
* Document important requirement decisions
* Improve communication around risks and blockers

---

# Sprint 2 — Payments and Shipping

### Sprint Goal

Improve the checkout experience by expanding payment options and giving customers greater control and visibility over shipping.

### Results

The team completed 18 of 23 planned story points.

### Key Impediment

The shipping provider API was not returning estimated delivery dates in the expected format.

The external provider also indicated that engineering support would not be available for several business days.

### Scrum Master Actions

I:

1. Made the dependency visible.
2. Confirmed the impact on the Sprint.
3. Facilitated communication with the external provider.
4. Communicated the potential impact to the Product Owner.
5. Discussed whether the affected story should remain in the Sprint.
6. Allowed the Developers to determine their technical approach.
7. Continued monitoring the dependency.

The Product Owner agreed that the affected story could move to a future Sprint if necessary.

---

# Sprint 2 Review

The Sprint Review produced additional stakeholder feedback.

### Marketing

Customers were asking for estimated delivery information.

### Operations

The Operations stakeholder wanted order tracking considered before additional checkout features.

### Product Owner

The Product Owner planned to move estimated delivery higher in the Product Backlog and investigate order tracking for future work.

### Scrum Master Learning

The Sprint Review demonstrated how stakeholder feedback can influence future Product Backlog decisions.

My responsibility was to facilitate transparency and discussion rather than decide the Product Backlog priorities myself.

# Key Lessons Learned

## 1. Facilitation is different from directing.

A Scrum Master should create an environment where the team can make effective decisions rather than making technical decisions for them.

## 2. Impediments should be made visible.

Dependencies that are hidden or discovered late can create unnecessary Sprint risk.

## 3. Refinement matters.

Clear requirements before Sprint Planning can reduce confusion and rework during the Sprint.

## 4. Transparency is important when work is blocked.

Instead of promising a completion date, the Scrum Master should communicate the actual situation and help the appropriate people determine the next step.

## 5. Velocity is a planning tool.

Velocity can provide historical information to help with forecasting and Sprint planning, but it should not be used to measure individual Developer performance.

## 6. Scrum roles need to remain clear.

The Product Owner owns Product Backlog ordering.

Developers determine how the work is completed and assess their capacity.

The Scrum Master facilitates, coaches, removes impediments, and supports Scrum effectiveness.

## Final Reflection

The biggest lesson from this simulation was that the Scrum Master role is not about managing Developers or telling the team what to do.

The role is about creating the conditions for the Scrum Team to work effectively.

Throughout the project, I practiced identifying impediments, facilitating difficult conversations, improving requirement clarity, communicating risks, supporting stakeholder feedback, and using Sprint data to improve future planning.

This simulation also identified areas I would continue developing through real-world Scrum experience, particularly working with complex dependencies, changing stakeholder needs, and evolving Product Backlogs.
